Use It Up

A recipe matcher that shows what you can cook with what's already in your kitchen, starting with the ingredients about to expire.

Problem

People overspend on groceries and throw out food they forgot they had. I'm one of them. Nothing tells you what you can make with what you have at the moment you're deciding what to eat.

Research

Informal interviews showed the friction isn't finding recipes. It's forgetting what's already in the fridge before a shopping trip.

Scope decision

I cut barcode scanning, price tracking and grocery lists from v1 to prove the core matching loop first. Grocery lists came back once it was clear they were pure logic with no outside dependency. A live recipe API and accounts stayed out, since neither was needed to test the core idea.

What I built

An ingredient matcher across 25 recipes and 14 cuisines that ranks by how many ingredients you have and moves recipes that rescue expiring food to the top. Missing ingredients from every planned recipe merge into one grocery list, sorted by aisle.

Shipped v1

Closet Match

Turns your Pinterest boards into outfits, then shows which pieces you already own and which ones you're missing.

Problem

People save hundreds of outfits on Pinterest and still feel like they have nothing to wear. The gap is between loving a look and knowing you can make it from your own closet.

Scope decision

Pinterest runs on a demo account in v1. Real sign-in needs a server to keep tokens out of the browser and an app review from Pinterest, so I built the product against one small interface the real connection can plug into later.

What I built

A board importer that reads each pin, tags it by style, occasion and clothing pieces, and matches it against your closet. It detects your top styles from what you pin, and every look explains why it was recommended.

Design decision

The page starts empty instead of filling up with stock photos. Every image comes from your own boards, so the product feels personal from the first click.
